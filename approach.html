My approach

How I work through a project.

I care about what happens after the first version works: whether someone understands the next step, whether their work is saved, and whether the app tells them something useful when it goes wrong.

Start with something a person needs to do

I find a project easier to judge when its purpose is concrete. In DataDock, that means taking a CSV from upload to a useful report. In SnapChef, it means turning a photo into a cooking plan someone can revisit. I use that path to decide which features help and which ones just add another screen.

Follow the data, not just the interface

A save button should have a clear meaning. DataDock reports belong to an account or guest session; SignalDesk progress stays in one browser. The project notes explain those choices, what gets sent to an external service, and what can disappear when browser storage is cleared.

Treat feedback as part of the build

Some of the most useful changes started with a very ordinary complaint: a sidebar was clipped, a status disappeared after refresh, or the portfolio said the same thing too many times. I want that feedback to lead to a specific change that can be checked, rather than another layer of polish over the same problem.

Use AI tools with a clear role

I use AI-assisted development tools for implementation support, refactoring, documentation, and testing. I set the product requirements and priorities, review how the result behaves, and decide what needs another revision. That includes choices about the interface, persistence, and how errors are explained. AI assistance is part of the workflow; the source, tests, and build notes make the implementation and its limits open to review.

Check the behavior that matters

A page loading successfully is only the start. The recent checks cover rejected uploads, failed requests, account isolation, saved history, and incident changes surviving refresh. Mocked service tests help catch regressions, while live checks confirm the deployed interface. Neither is a reason to claim that every provider or device has been tested.

Be specific about contributions

The individual projects and the team bookstore have different histories. On the bookstore, my documented course contribution focused on workflow diagrams and testable requirements. I keep that separate from describing what the complete application does. The same principle applies to AI assistance and deployment templates: say what is there, how it works, and what was actually delivered.
