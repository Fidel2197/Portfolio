Students brought together arrays, keyboard input, game loops, rotations, collisions, row clearing, and scoring in a browser game. The goal was for the student to understand why it failed and apply the same reasoning to the next problem.

Aug 2023 – May 2024Robotics integration
Prototype testing

Lab Research Assistant

Department of Computing and Engineering · McNeese State University

Assembled and tested robotic prototypes with faculty and other researchers. I checked motors, sensors, wiring, calibration, and control logic, then documented changes between test runs.

  • Repeated testing: evaluated movement accuracy, motor response, sensor reliability, timing, positioning, and autonomous behavior. I compared commanded actions with the robot’s physical response and repeated tests to see whether a problem was consistent.
  • Isolating failures: checked connections, power delivery, calibration, mechanical resistance, sensor placement, and control logic. Testing a motor or sensor on its own helped distinguish a hardware issue from a problem in the full movement sequence.
  • Recording iterations: kept notes on configurations, test conditions, observed problems, modifications, and results. After an adjustment, I reran the same checks so the team could compare performance and reproduce earlier configurations.
A closer look: an inconsistent sensor

When readings were unreliable, I helped test the sensor under different conditions, check its connections, and adjust its position or calibration. Evaluating that input separately from the robot’s decision-making logic helped narrow down the cause before changing the wider system.

I also helped prepare and demonstrate the prototypes at McNeese Engineering Week for more than 50 attendees. That involved checking the systems before the demonstrations and explaining how the hardware, software, testing, and calibration worked together to people without a robotics background.
