Aug 2023 – May 2024Programming support
Testing and feedback

Teaching Assistant

Department of Computing · McNeese State University

I supported more than 30 undergraduate students in weekly JavaScript, HTML, and introductory web development labs. A lot of conversations started with “my program doesn’t work.” I would work through the student’s own code with them, reproduce the problem, and help them find the cause without taking over the assignment.

  • Debugging in the browser: used Visual Studio Code, browser developer tools, console errors, and logging to trace variable values, conditions, loops, functions, arrays, event listeners, and DOM updates.
  • Testing student work: ran and evaluated more than 100 assignments and projects, comparing actual behavior with the requirements. I checked navigation, buttons, forms, input validation, and dynamic page behavior, then explained the issues in written and verbal feedback.
  • Development setup: helped resolve incorrect file paths, JavaScript files not loading, browser caching, missing resources, and file-organization problems that stopped students from running their work.

A closer look: the Tetris labs

Students brought together arrays, keyboard input, game loops, rotations, collisions, row clearing, and scoring in a browser game. If a piece moved outside the board or a row failed to clear, I helped isolate that section and test its logic. The goal was for the student to understand why it failed and apply the same reasoning to the next problem.

I also maintained assignment and grade information in Excel, worked with course documents in Word, and communicated through Outlook. During one-on-one support, I adjusted the explanation to the student—sometimes tracing a program line by line, sometimes breaking an assignment into smaller tasks.

Aug 2023 – May 2024Robotics integration
Prototype testing

Lab Research Assistant

Department of Computing and Engineering · McNeese State University

I worked with faculty and other researchers to assemble, integrate, test, and troubleshoot robotic prototypes. The systems combined motors, sensors, electronics, mechanical assemblies, wiring, and programmed control logic. My work included checking how those pieces behaved separately and what changed when they operated together.

  • Repeated testing: evaluated movement accuracy, motor response, sensor reliability, timing, positioning, and autonomous behavior. I compared commanded actions with the robot’s physical response and repeated tests to see whether a problem was consistent.
  • Isolating failures: checked connections, power delivery, calibration, mechanical resistance, sensor placement, and control logic. Testing a motor or sensor on its own helped distinguish a hardware issue from a problem in the full movement sequence.
  • Recording iterations: kept notes on configurations, test conditions, observed problems, modifications, and results. After an adjustment, I reran the same checks so the team could compare performance and reproduce earlier configurations.

A closer look: an inconsistent sensor

When readings were unreliable, I helped test the sensor under different conditions, check its connections, and adjust its position or calibration. Evaluating that input separately from the robot’s decision-making logic helped narrow down the cause before changing the wider system.

I also helped prepare and demonstrate the prototypes at McNeese Engineering Week for more than 50 attendees. That involved checking the systems before the demonstrations and explaining how the hardware, software, testing, and calibration worked together to people without a robotics background.

Aug 2024 – PresentResident support
Community operations

Resident Assistant

McNeese State University Housing

I support a residential community of more than 50 students and serve as a point of contact between residents and housing staff. The work ranges from explaining a housing procedure to helping someone work through a roommate concern or find the right campus resource.

  • Duty and follow-through: complete community rounds, respond to concerns outside normal office hours, gather relevant information, and communicate with professional staff. I document what happened and follow up so the next action does not get lost between people or shifts.
  • Room and community inspections: review living conditions, record observations, explain required corrections, and carry out follow-up procedures. I keep records of inspections, incidents, resident communication, and duty activities.
  • Team coordination: work with other RAs and Residence Life staff on duty coverage, resident concerns, training, larger events, and move-in and move-out responsibilities. Consistent information matters when several staff members are involved.

Taking a program from idea to completion

I plan social, academic, cultural, and wellness activities, including game nights, movie nights, gratitude activities, and academic support events. That includes preparing materials, coordinating logistics, advertising, setting up, hosting, and completing the post-program documentation.

This role calls for calm, objective communication—especially around disagreements, maintenance concerns, shared-space expectations, and housing policies. Listening carefully and knowing when to involve the right staff member are as important as answering the first question.
